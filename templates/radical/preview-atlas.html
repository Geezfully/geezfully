Start here

studio · The practice

We were founded in 2018 after a transport authority asked us why a redesigned ticketing system had made queues longer. The answer took four months and involved standing in stations at six in the morning. That is still roughly the method.

We publish everything we can. Where a client will not let us publish the work, we publish the method.

Why the queue got longer

project · Transport authority · 2025 · 7 months

The new machines were faster per transaction and slower per passenger. People arrived in groups, and the old machines allowed one person to buy for four. The new flow did not, so a group of four became four queue positions.

We recommended a group-purchase path and a physical reorganisation of the concourse. Queue length fell 38% within a month of the change, before any software was rewritten.

The form nobody finished

project · Housing department · 2024 · 11 months

The original form asked for a National Insurance number on page one. Roughly a third of applicants did not have it in front of them, left to find it, and never returned.

We moved identity verification to the end, made every field saveable, and cut the form from 44 questions to 19 by taking the rest from records the department already held. Completion rose to 81%.

Six minutes is the cliff

finding · Finding · across four studies

This holds for a benefits form, a vehicle registration, a school application and a clinical questionnaire. It appears to be about the length of an uninterrupted moment in an ordinary day, not about the difficulty of the task.

The practical version: if your service takes longer than six minutes, it must survive being abandoned and resumed. Most cannot.

Reading a hospital at 3am

project · NHS trust · 2024 · 9 months

We interviewed 60 people within a week of an unplanned hospital visit. Almost none remembered reading a sign. They remembered following someone, asking someone, or a colour.

The redesign leant on colour and floor markings rather than text, and put one staffed point in the entrance rather than four unstaffed signboards. Time from door to correct department fell by a third.

On watching instead of asking

note · Field note · March 2026

We ask afterwards, never instead. The pattern of a study is: watch for a week without talking to anyone, then interview using what you saw as the prompt.

It is slower and it embarrasses everyone slightly, and it is the only reason our recommendations survive contact with a real building.

The pilot that never ends

note · Field note · January 2026

Three of our early projects ended as permanent pilots — running, funded, unloved and unscaled. The fix was procedural, not analytical: we agree in advance what result would justify rolling out, and what result would justify stopping.

What we will not do

finding · Method

Each of these is a way of getting a number quickly that will not survive the building it is meant to describe. We will use all three as supporting evidence and none of them as the argument.

This costs us about one commission a year, which we consider a reasonable price.
